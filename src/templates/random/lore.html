lore

(The questions I get asked, and the answers I actually give)

life

So tell me about yourself
+

Hi! I'm a master's student with a growing interest in EDA. I enjoy breaking down complicated problems and turning them into practical solutions. Beyond making things work, I also care about making them clear, approachable, and enjoyable for people to use.

Why EDA?
+

It started in my senior year, when my professor invited me to join the ICCAD CAD Contest. Our team received an Honorable Mention, and somewhere between the algorithms, optimization problems, and long debugging sessions, I realized this was something I genuinely wanted to keep doing.

Our problem was a chip-level global router. Here is a much smaller version of the same idea: connect the two pins, route around the blockages, and try not to waste wire.

What is your biggest weakness?
+

I can spend an unreasonable amount of time trying to make something just a little bit better. Sometimes "good enough" arrives before I am emotionally ready to accept it.

What do you care about when building something?
+

First, I care about impact: does it solve a real problem and produce a meaningful result? Then comes the user experience. A technically strong project is more valuable when people can understand and use it naturally.

I also put a lot of thought into UI design — I want interfaces to feel clean, intuitive, and visually polished, without getting in the user's way.

building

What are you working with these days?
+

Mostly C++, TCL, and AI-agent co-work. I like treating agents as collaborators: they can help with research, implementation, debugging, and the occasional "why is this failing?" existential crisis.

What does "agent co-work" mean to you?
+

It is not about asking AI to do everything for me. It is about using it to explore ideas faster, remove repetitive work, find useful resources, and leave more time for the parts that still need human judgment.

How do you learn new things?
+

YouTube first. Some creators explain difficult topics so clearly that it feels like cheating. When YouTube does not have the answer, I ask AI to help me find better resources — or teach me directly if the question is small enough.

What have you built before?
+

I built mspice, a python-based SPICE simulation project for my undergraduate capstone, and WeiStudio, where I share some of my past Photoshop and Illustrator work.

Both projects gave me a chance to put serious effort into web design, not just functionality, and create experiences that feel clear, intentional, and visually appealing.

What kind of problems do you enjoy solving?
+

Problems where there is both a technical challenge and a real payoff. If a solution can be faster, clearer, and make someone's life easier, that is usually a good sign I will enjoy working on it.

What is your definition of a successful project?
+

Something that works, helps someone, and does not make its users ask "Why is it like this?" within the first five minutes.

random

What do you do outside of work and school?
+

Skiing when I get the chance, playing Arena of Valor or Brawl Stars when I should probably be doing something else, and occasionally convincing myself that one more match is a reasonable decision.

What are you looking forward to next?
+

Going back to Japan to ski. Mountains, snow, good food, and temporarily having a valid excuse not to open my laptop sounds pretty perfect.

What is your ideal debugging environment?
+

A working internet connection, something to drink, and a bug that is not caused by one missing semicolon. I am flexible on the rest.

What is your relationship with C++?
+

Complicated. It gives me performance, control, and algorithms to think about — then occasionally reminds me that memory has feelings too.
